# Referee reports, associate editor letters, and cover letters to editors

Read this for any reviewing work. The core rules in SKILL.md still apply. Reports and AE letters use no contractions and no irony.

## How he criticizes (applies to all three)

**Conceptual critique first.** His deepest criticisms target conceptual foundations, not execution. The pattern: state what the paper does, identify the conceptual gap at the core, demonstrate it precisely, then list specific issues. He does not lead with typos or exposition complaints.

**Demonstrate, don't assert.** When he says a metric, argument, or claim is wrong, he works through the numbers or the logic to show it. Examples: computing the majority-voting probability to show groups are not more rational (it matches the data without the authors' conclusion); showing that (0,0,100,100) gets the same score as (41,41,41,41) under a proposed metric; walking through a sequence of prices to show the market maker update logic fails. When editing, if a criticism is asserted without demonstration, ask whether a concrete example would make it sharper. Often it will.

**Criticism as a question.** "Why are these thresholds chosen?" "What does it mean that (0,0,100,100) performs better than (41,41,41,41)?" This invites the authors to fix the problem rather than just defend themselves.

**Precise and technical, never personal.**

**The "I" pattern.** "I think," "I find," "I cannot convince myself," "I must add," "I realize." This separates a judgment call from a mathematical fact. Keep it; it is his voice and it is intellectually honest.

## Phrases to preserve

Do not normalize these away.

- **"I cannot convince myself that..."** Distinctively his. Use it when a conclusion is not supported by the evidence or argument, not as a general expression of doubt.
- **"I must, however, add that..."** A gentle pivot that signals the real point is coming.
- **"Let me explain."** Follows a claim that needs unpacking.
- **"I truly enjoyed reading the paper."** Used selectively, when genuine. It earns credibility because sharp criticism follows.
- **"I am afraid I must say..."** Signals rejection-level criticism. Not dramatic, just honest.
- **"This is a nice attempt to..."** Warm framing before a substantive reservation.

## Referee reports

**Opening:** a neutral summary of the paper, two to four sentences, no judgment. Then one sentence of overall verdict, almost always balanced: credit first, reservation second. "This is a nice paper. The results are of interest... However, I cannot convince myself that the results are novel enough to warrant publication in a top general interest journal." "I find the main questions studied in this paper novel and interesting. However, I am equally concerned about the execution."

**Warmth:** brief and genuine. "This is a nice paper." "This is a neat setup." "I hope the authors find them useful to improve the impact of the paper." Warmth never softens a technical point; it frames the relationship as collegial.

**Organization:** by substance, not by paper section. No section-by-section walkthrough; bold labels are fine for clearly distinct concerns. It ends when the substantive points are made, with no summary or closing flourish.

## Associate editor letters

He is managing the process here, not just evaluating the paper.

**Structure:** (1) Report what the referee said and the overall recommendation. (2) His own additional comments. (3) A clear procedural outcome.

**Opener:** "I have received a report from a referee who [characterization]. Having read the paper, I [concur/disagree]. I recommend [decision]." Direct, procedural, no preamble.

**Tone:** warmer and more authoritative than a referee report. Not adversarial; helping the paper get better. "I will leave it to the authors to decide."

**Closing:** always state the procedural outcome. "Accept after revision." "I do not need to see the manuscript again." "I invite the authors to revise and resubmit."

## Cover letters to editors

The most informal register in his academic writing: a personal note to a named editor, not an institutional document. Contractions are fine.

**Structure:** (1) One sentence identifying the paper, with a warm orienting comment. (2) A compact summary of what the paper does and where it stands, essentially a mini-verdict with reasoning. (3) A brief recommendation. (4) A warm closing.

**Opener:** first name if he knows the editor ("Dear Matt,"). No "Dear Professor."

**The mini-summary** is the key section: the contribution, its relation to prior literature, and his reservation in a few plain sentences. He does not withhold the verdict. "I am afraid the results are not general enough to warrant publication in Econometrica." He says it, then explains why.

**Tone:** collegial, honest, slightly warm. He thanks the editor without being effusive. "I hope you find my comments helpful." "I appreciate the extra time you gave me."

**Sign-off:** "All the best," then "Boğaç." This is the one context where "All the best" replaces "Best,".

**Avoid:** a formal letter with a long opening. No "I am writing to inform you..." or "Please find enclosed..."
